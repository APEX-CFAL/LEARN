# Garden Performing Stage with Mud Brick Backdrop Wall
CFAL campus garden · Design brief · 1 October 2026

## 1. What the site gives us
- A mature tree (mango-like canopy) standing at the back edge of a raised earthen terrace. The terrace already sits one step above the lawn, with a low stone kerb along its front edge.
- A flat lawn in front, sloping gently away. This is the natural audience space.
- A building behind with an artificial green screen and jaali blocks. The wall should hide this.
- A stone stepping path on the right, a chain-link fence on the right, a leaf heap on the left.
- The tree is the strongest thing on the site. Design around it, not against it.

## 2. Concept in one line
A low earth-coloured stage on the existing terrace, with the tree as a living centrepiece and a two-winged mud brick wall behind it that hides the building and frames the performer.

## 3. Layout (plan)
- Stage: the existing terrace, levelled and paved. Target 7 m wide x 4 m deep. Height 450 mm above lawn (one comfortable step). Keep the existing kerb line as the stage lip.
- Tree: stays at back-centre of the stage. A 1.5 m radius around the trunk stays unpaved (gravel or the existing stone ring) so roots breathe and water reaches them.
- Wall: two wings, each about 3 m long, 2.4 m high, set 2.5 to 3 m behind the trunk line. The gap between the wings lets the trunk and canopy read as part of the backdrop. The gap also gives performers a rear entry.
- Wings angled 10 to 15 degrees inwards towards the audience. This cups the sound and makes the stage feel held.
- Steps: one broad step (300 mm tread) along the full front edge, or two sets of three steps at the corners. A full-width step doubles as a front-row seat for small children.
- Audience: lawn as is. Later, two or three shallow laterite terraces cut into the slope would seat 150 people without any chairs.

## 4. The mud brick wall (the part that must be done right)
Mangalore gets 3,500 mm of rain a year. Earth walls survive this only with a good hat and good boots.

Boots (plinth)
- Laterite block plinth, 600 mm above finished stage level, 400 mm wide, on a 300 mm rubble or PCC footing.
- Damp-proof course (bitumen felt or a thin cement-sand layer) on top of the plinth before the first earth course.

Body
- Compressed Stabilised Earth Blocks (CSEB), 5 to 8 percent cement, 240 x 240 x 90 mm, laid in earth-cement mortar. Adobe (unstabilised sun-dried brick) is possible but will need a full lime plaster and yearly care. CSEB is the honest choice for this climate.
- Wall thickness 240 mm. Height 2.4 m above plinth (about 24 courses). Both wings tied with a reinforced concrete or stabilised-earth band at the top.
- Finish: exposed CSEB inside the wings so the earth is visible from the audience. Lime wash or a thin earth-lime plaster on the back face if weather hits it.

Hat (top protection)
- A light roof strip along each wing: Mangalore clay tiles on a timber or steel frame, 600 mm overhang to the front and 400 mm to the back. Or a simple sloped laterite coping with a 100 mm drip if a roof feels too much. Tiles are better.
- Rain from the roof must not splash the wall foot. Run a 300 mm gravel strip along the base.

Details worth adding
- Two or three square niches (300 x 300 x 150 mm) in each wing for oil lamps or small sculptures.
- One recessed slot per wing for a hanging textile or banner during events.
- Conduit buried in the wall for two warm LED uplights at the base of each wing. Light the wall, not the audience.

## 5. Stage floor
- Compact the terrace, add 100 mm rubble, 50 mm sand, then laterite slabs or red oxide cement finish. Red oxide is cheaper and quieter underfoot. Laterite looks right next to the wall.
- Slope the floor 1 in 100 towards the front so monsoon water runs off onto the lawn.
- Fit a brass or stone marker at centre stage. Small thing, but performers love it.

## 6. Rough quantities
| Item | Quantity |
|---|---|
| CSEB blocks (two wings, 240 mm thick, with waste) | about 750 |
| Laterite blocks, plinth and coping | about 90 |
| Laterite paving, stage floor | about 28 sq m |
| Mangalore tiles, roof strips | about 300 |
| Timber or MS frame for roof strips | 2 nos, 3.2 m each |

Ask a local mason or the Auroville Earth Institute network for CSEB supply. Blocks can also be pressed on site with a hand press if the soil test passes.

## 7. Things to check before building
1. Where the afternoon sun falls. The audience should not face the setting sun. If it does, swap which side gets the taller wing or shift the stage 2 m.
2. Tree health. One limb on the right shows an old cut. Have an arborist look before you put people under it regularly.
3. Root zone. No footing within 2.5 m of the trunk. If the wall must come closer, bridge the footing over roots instead of cutting them.
4. Drainage off the terrace. Water currently pools where the stage will be. Check after one heavy shower.
5. Remove the artificial green screen once the wall is up. The mud wall makes it unnecessary.

## 8. Phasing
- Phase 1 (dry season, 3 weeks): level and pave the stage, build the plinth, lay the step.
- Phase 2 (2 weeks): raise the CSEB wings, top band, roof strips.
- Phase 3 (1 week): lights, niches, gravel strip, planting at the wall base (lemongrass or vetiver, both low and fragrant).
- Hold the first performance before the monsoon. Watch how the wall weathers through one monsoon before adding plaster anywhere.
